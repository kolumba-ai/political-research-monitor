4 · Design · System D

Each claim is polled only until its sample is valid

30 respondents first, then batches of 20. No claim stops before every population group has 5 answers.

StatusPolling stops when
ConvergedThe share who believe the claim is known to within ±7 points (95% confidence)
DecisivePolled sentiment clearly differs from social-media sentiment
Capped400 completed answers: inconclusive, the question is rewritten

What simulation found

11–19%

of claims with no real gap would be reported as decisive, because the check repeats after every batch.

Fix: test at 99% confidence, which brings it to 3–5%. A small gap is then caught 64% of the time instead of 85%.

Source: Monte Carlo simulation of the stopping rule, 20,000 runs per scenario (System D test plan)

System D polls in batches and stops each claim as soon as the answer is statistically valid, which keeps cost down. We simulated the rule before building it. Because the decisive check runs after every batch, it would report a gap that isn’t there 11 to 19 percent of the time, not 5. Testing that one check at 99 percent confidence fixes it, at the cost of missing some small gaps. The test plan has five more fixes like this.
