3 · Methodology

From collective claims to evidence to polls

1

Who holds office

Every officeholder is checked against fetched pages: two independent domains, or one official source.

2

What is claimed

Posts by and about each politician on TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, X and Facebook, plus media coverage.

3

What the evidence says

News, statistics, official and court records, each marked as supporting or contradicting a claim, with a verified quote.

4

What people believe

A stratified sample is polled on each claim, and polled sentiment is compared with social-media sentiment.

“We collect evidence, not verdicts. Polls decide whether belief follows the evidence.”

Sources: project principles · More in Common, The Perception Gap (2019)

Four steps. First, who holds office, verified against the pages themselves. Second, what is claimed by and about them across social media and the media. Third, what the evidence says, with each document marked as supporting or contradicting the claim and the quote checked against the page. Fourth, what people believe, by polling. The project never issues verdicts on its own; polls close the loop. The comparison borrows its method from More in Common’s Perception Gap study: measure what people believe separately from what is said about them.
